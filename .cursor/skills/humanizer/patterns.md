# Humanizer patterns (reference)

Condensed from [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) v2.11.2. Read when the quick scan in SKILL.md is not enough.

## Content patterns

1. **Inflated importance** — "pivotal moment", "testament to", "underscores the significance"
2. **Name-dropping** — citation lists without context
3. **Shallow -ing phrases** — "highlighting", "ensuring", "reflecting"
4. **Sales language** — "nestled", "vibrant", "breathtaking", "showcases"
5. **Vague sources** — "experts argue", "industry reports"
6. **Stock challenge sections** — "Despite these challenges... continues to thrive"

## Language patterns

7. **Overused AI words** — additionally, crucial, delve, fostering, landscape, pivotal, showcase, testament, underscore, vibrant
8. **Avoiding is/are** — "serves as", "boasts", "features" instead of "is"/"has"
9. **Not X but Y** — "It's not just X, it's Y"
10. **Forced triplets** — three parallel items when two suffice
11. **Synonym cycling** — renaming the same subject every sentence
12. **False ranges** — "from X to Y" when X and Y are not a range
13. **Passive voice** — missing actor ("Results are preserved" → who preserves them?)

## Style patterns

14. **Em/en dashes** — replace with comma, period, or parentheses unless writer sample uses dashes
15. **Excess bold** — decorative bold in prose
16. **Bold mini-headings in lists** — `- **Label:** text` → merge into prose
17. **Title case headings** — use sentence case
18. **Emojis in headings**
19. **Curly quotes** — use straight quotes in technical docs

## Chatbot patterns

20. **Leftover chatbot text** — "Here is...", "Let me know if..."
21. **Knowledge disclaimers** — "as of my last update", speculative gap-fill
22. **Overly agreeable tone** — "Great question!"

## Filler patterns

23. **Filler phrases** — "in order to", "due to the fact that", "it is important to note"
24. **Qualifier piles** — "could potentially possibly"
25. **Generic positive endings** — "exciting times lie ahead"
26. **Hyphen overuse** — hyphenate only before nouns when grammar requires it
27. **Fake depth** — "the real question is", "at its core"
28. **Announcements** — "Let's dive in", "Here's what you need to know"
29. **Heading restatement** — first sentence repeats the heading
30. **Writing about old versions** — describe current behavior in reference docs
31. **Dramatic fragments** — stacked one-line sentences for effect
32. **Formulaic sayings** — "X is the language of Y"
33. **Fake-candid openings** — "Honestly?", "Here's the thing"
34. **Answering unraised objections** — "I'm not saying..."
35. **Rejecting fake alternatives** — straw-man options dismissed in passing

## False positives (do not flag alone)

Polish, formal words, one em dash, one "additionally", curly quotes from editors, deliberate repetition for rhythm.

## Human details to keep

Specific odd details, mixed feelings, genuine asides, varied sentence length, deliberate first-person where appropriate.
